Step Zero

Is the Project Eligible?

VM0044 sets clear applicability conditions. We screen every candidate site against them before any further investment is made.

🌾

Feedstock

  • Waste or residual biomass: crop residues, forestry and sawmill residues, prunings, husks, shells
  • Sustainable sourcing documented, with no competing use displaced
  • Biomass grown on land cleared or converted for the project
  • Contaminated or treated wood and mixed municipal waste
🔥

Production Technology

  • Pyrolysis or gasification in an oxygen-limited environment
  • Process temperature monitored and recorded for every batch
  • Pyrolysis gases combusted or captured, not vented
  • Facility permitted and operated to local environmental rules
🧪

Biochar Quality

  • Molar H:Corg ratio below 0.7
  • Organic carbon content measured by accredited laboratory
  • Heavy metals and PAHs within IBI / EBC or national limits
  • Representative sampling per production batch
📍

End Use

  • Applied to soil (agriculture, horticulture, land restoration)
  • Or incorporated into durable non-soil products (e.g. concrete, asphalt)
  • End-use traceable through delivery and application records
  • Combustion or use as fuel at any point
Biochar Workplan

From Site to Issued Credits

Our six-phase development workplan for every NatWorks biochar project. Each phase has defined activities, deliverables, and a go / no-go gate.

  1. 01

    Feasibility & Feedstock Assessment

    1–2 months

    Confirm that enough eligible biomass exists, that it can be collected economically, and that the project passes VM0044 applicability.

    Activities

    • Biomass resource mapping and seasonal availability
    • Competing-use and leakage screening
    • Site, logistics and offtake (end-user) scoping
    • Preliminary removal and revenue model

    Deliverables

    • Feasibility report
    • Feedstock inventory and sourcing plan
    • Eligibility checklist and go / no-go decision
  2. 02

    Project Design & Documentation

    2–4 months

    Design the production system and write the Project Description (PD) that defines boundaries, baseline, additionality and monitoring.

    Activities

    • Pyrolysis technology selection and sizing
    • Project boundary, baseline and additionality analysis
    • Monitoring plan and sampling protocol design
    • Local stakeholder consultation and safeguards review

    Deliverables

    • VCS Project Description
    • Emission-reduction calculation workbook
    • Monitoring plan and SOPs
    • Verra pipeline listing
  3. 03

    Validation & Registration

    3–6 months

    An accredited Validation/Verification Body (VVB) independently assesses the PD against VCS rules and VM0044 before Verra registers the project.

    Activities

    • VVB selection and contracting
    • Document review and site visit
    • Responding to findings (CARs / CLs)
    • Public comment period on the Verra registry

    Deliverables

    • Validation report
    • Registered project on Verra registry
  4. 04

    Production & Application

    Ongoing

    Commission the facility, train operators, and run the feedstock → pyrolysis → biochar → end-use supply chain under the monitoring plan.

    Activities

    • Facility commissioning and operator training
    • Batch production with temperature logging
    • Batch sampling and laboratory analysis
    • Delivery to and application by farmers / end users

    Deliverables

    • Batch production records
    • Lab certificates of analysis
    • Delivery and application records
  5. 05

    Monitoring & Reporting

    Each monitoring period

    Collect and quality-check all monitored parameters, calculate net removals, and compile the monitoring report for the period.

    Activities

    • Data QA/QC and reconciliation of mass balances
    • Project emissions and leakage calculation
    • Permanence factor application
    • Internal audit before submission

    Deliverables

    • Monitoring report
    • Updated calculation workbook
    • Evidence pack for the VVB
  6. 06

    Verification & Issuance

    2–4 months per cycle

    A VVB verifies the monitoring report. Verra then issues Verified Carbon Units (VCUs) for the net removals, ready for sale or retirement.

    Activities

    • Verification audit and site visit
    • Closing findings
    • Issuance request to Verra
    • Buyer reporting and credit retirement support

    Deliverables

    • Verification report
    • VCUs issued on the Verra registry

Typical time to first issuance: 12–18 months from feasibility start. Durations are indicative and depend on facility build time, VVB availability, and registry review queues.

Quantification

How Carbon Removal Is Calculated

Credits are issued only for the carbon that stays locked in biochar over the long term, after every project-related emission is deducted.

CR = Σ ( Mbiochar × Corg × PF ) × 44⁄12 − PE − LE

Simplified form of the VM0044 net removal equation, summed over all biochar batches in a monitoring period (tCO₂e).

Mbiochar

Biochar Mass

Dry mass of biochar produced and delivered to an eligible end use in each batch, measured on calibrated scales and adjusted for moisture.

Corg

Organic Carbon Content

Fraction of the biochar's dry mass that is organic carbon, from accredited lab analysis of representative batch samples.

PF

Permanence Factor

Share of biochar carbon expected to remain after 100 years. It depends on H:Corg ratio and the soil temperature at the application site.

44⁄12

Carbon to CO₂

Converts tonnes of carbon to tonnes of CO₂ using the molecular-weight ratio of CO₂ to C.

PE

Project Emissions

Emissions from feedstock collection and transport, energy and fuel use, CH₄ and N₂O from pyrolysis, and transport of biochar to the end user.

LE

Leakage

Emissions caused outside the project boundary, for example if diverting biomass forces its previous users to switch to fossil alternatives.

Conservative baseline: in the baseline scenario the residual biomass would decompose or be burned, returning its carbon to the atmosphere. Removals are therefore credited only for the stable biochar carbon. No credit is claimed for avoided burning or soil co-benefits.

Permanence

Built to Last Centuries

Biochar's aromatic carbon structure decomposes extremely slowly, but not at zero. VM0044 accounts for this with a permanence factor that discounts the small share of carbon expected to be lost over a 100-year horizon.

Two variables drive the factor. The first is the H:Corg ratio: a lower ratio means a more condensed, stable structure, driven mainly by pyrolysis temperature. The second is the soil temperature where the biochar is applied: warmer soils mean faster microbial decay. NatWorks tightly controls the first and records the second for every application site.

We target pyrolysis temperatures above 500 °C so that H:Corg sits well below the 0.7 eligibility limit. This maximises the permanence factor and the credits each tonne of biochar earns.

How permanence drivers affect crediting
Driver Effect on PF How we manage it
H:Corg ratio Lower ratio → higher PF Temperature-controlled pyrolysis, batch lab testing
Pyrolysis temperature Higher temp → lower H:Corg Continuous thermocouple logging
Soil temperature Cooler soils → higher PF Site-specific climate data per application area
End use Durable products may differ from soil Application records and end-user agreements
Reversal risk Combustion would release carbon Contractual no-burn commitments, traceability
Monitoring, Reporting & Verification

What We Monitor

Every credited tonne traces back to measured data, from the field where biomass is collected to the field where biochar is applied.

🚜

Feedstock Intake

Type, source, mass and moisture of every feedstock delivery; collection distances.

Every delivery
🌡️

Process Conditions

Pyrolysis temperature and residence time, start-up fuel, electricity use.

Continuous / per batch
⚖️

Biochar Output

Mass and moisture of biochar produced, with a unique batch ID for traceability.

Per batch
🔬

Lab Analysis

Organic carbon, H:Corg, ash, heavy metals and PAHs by an accredited laboratory.

Per sampling plan
🚚

Distribution

Delivered quantities, transport mode and distance to each end user.

Every delivery
📍

Application

GPS location, date, area and application rate; signed end-user declarations.

Every application

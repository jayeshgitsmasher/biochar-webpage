Definition

What is Biochar?

Biochar is a carbon-rich solid material produced by the thermochemical conversion of organic matter — biomass — in an oxygen-limited environment through a process called pyrolysis. The term is a compound of "biological" and "charcoal", reflecting both its organic origins and its charcoal-like properties.

Unlike charcoal used for cooking, biochar is specifically engineered for soil application and carbon sequestration. Its distinctive porous microstructure — visible under a microscope as a honeycomb of interconnected channels — provides the structural basis for its resistance to decomposition and its beneficial interactions with soil biology.

The International Biochar Initiative (IBI) defines biochar as "a solid material obtained from the thermochemical conversion of biomass in an oxygen-limited environment" that meets minimum quality standards for soil application.

The Process

Pyrolysis: Controlled Thermal Conversion

Pyrolysis is the thermochemical decomposition of organic material at elevated temperatures (350–700°C) in the absence or severe restriction of oxygen. This distinguishes it from combustion (which requires oxygen and produces CO₂) and from composting (which is biological and slow).

🌾

Feedstock Preparation

Biomass (rice husks, wheat straw, wood chips, bamboo) is dried to <20% moisture and sized for uniform processing. Feedstock quality determines biochar quality.

→
🔥

Thermal Decomposition

At 200–300°C, moisture evaporates and volatile organic compounds release. At 350–500°C, cellulose and hemicellulose break down. At 500–700°C, lignin converts to stable aromatic carbon.

→
⚗️

Gas Separation

Volatile pyrolysis gases (syngas) and bio-oil vapors are separated and can be used for process heat. The remaining solid is raw biochar char.

→
🌡️

Cooling & Testing

Biochar is cooled under inert conditions, then tested for carbon content (>50% organic C), H:Corg ratio (<0.7 for stability), pH, and contaminants.

→
✅

Quality Certification

Certified biochar meeting IBI or EBC standards with documented production parameters for MRV under VM0044.

Permanence

Carbon Permanence Across Storage Types

The defining advantage of biochar over other carbon removal approaches is its extraordinary carbon stability. While plant-based carbon in wood or soil organic matter typically cycles back to CO₂ within decades, biochar's aromatic carbon structure resists biological and chemical breakdown for hundreds to thousands of years.

H:Corg ratio < 0.7 is the key stability threshold specified by VM0044. Biochar produced at temperatures above 450°C consistently achieves this ratio, ensuring the sequestered carbon will remain stable for centuries under soil conditions.

Co-Benefits

Beyond Carbon: Regenerative Co-Benefits

Biochar's value proposition extends far beyond carbon sequestration. Its physical structure and chemical properties interact with soil, water, and biology to produce a cascade of co-benefits that make it unique among carbon removal approaches.

🌱

Soil Health Restoration

Biochar's porous structure improves soil aggregate stability, aeration, and tilth. Degraded and compacted soils show measurable improvements in physical properties within one growing season.

💧

Water Retention

Each gram of biochar can hold several times its weight in water. In sandy or drought-prone soils, biochar additions reduce irrigation requirements by 15–30%, critical for water-scarce regions.

🌾

Crop Yield Improvement

Field trials across Asia and Africa consistently show 10–40% yield improvements on degraded soils where biochar is applied, with larger gains on highly depleted soils.

♻️

Waste Stream Valorization

Agricultural residues that would be openly burned — releasing CO₂, black carbon, and particulates — are converted into a productive soil amendment, eliminating a significant local pollution source.

🌿

Methane & N₂O Prevention

Biochar alters soil microbial communities in ways that suppress methanogenic archaea and denitrifying bacteria, reducing soil methane and nitrous oxide emissions — both potent greenhouse gases.

👨‍🌾

Rural Livelihoods

Biochar supply chains create employment in biomass collection, processing, quality testing, and logistics, generating rural income in often marginal agricultural communities.

Standards

Verra VM0044: How It Works

Verra's VM0044 — Methodology for Biochar Carbon Dioxide Removal — is the definitive quantification and verification framework for biochar CDR. Released in 2021, it established the first rigorous accounting system for biochar as a carbon removal pathway.

VM0044 covers the entire biochar lifecycle: from biomass sourcing and production process monitoring, through biochar characterisation and quality verification, to application tracking and permanence accounting. It requires that every batch of biochar be characterised for its organic carbon content and stability ratio (H:Corg).

📊

Permanence Factor

VM0044 applies a permanence discount based on H:Corg ratio and temperature profile, accounting for the small fraction of biochar carbon that may decompose within the crediting period.

🔍

Batch-Level MRV

Each production batch is weighed, sampled, and lab-analysed. Production records and logistics are audited. Application sites are GPS-documented and monitored.

🚫

Leakage Accounting

The methodology quantifies and deducts all project-related emissions: energy used in pyrolysis, transport emissions, and foregone use of the biomass feedstock.

✅

Third-Party Verification

Independent auditors accredited by Verra conduct periodic verification audits, reviewing production data, lab results, and application records before carbon credits are issued.
